# Write a thank-you that names the specific contribution rather than the good intention

## Purpose

Write a thank-you that names the specific contribution rather than the good intention.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to thank a colleague in writing and have it land.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for formal published documentation; use technical-writing instead.

## Inputs required

- The audience and their prior knowledge
- The message and the outcome wanted
- The channel and the deadline

## Procedure

1. Name the specific thing the person did.
2. Describe what it made possible for the team or the work.
3. Keep the praise about the action rather than about the person's personality.
4. Make it short, because a long thank-you reads as an obligation.
5. Send it soon after the contribution, while it is still fresh.

## Output contract

A message ready to send, with the ask, the context and the next step made explicit.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The ask is stated in one sentence
- [ ] The tone matches the audience and channel
- [ ] The next step and its owner are clear

## Safety notes

Never send a message on the user's behalf without explicit approval of the final text.
