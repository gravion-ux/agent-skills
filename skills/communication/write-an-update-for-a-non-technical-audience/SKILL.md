# Explain a technical update to a non-technical audience in terms of what it means for them

## Purpose

Explain a technical update to a non-technical audience in terms of what it means for them.

## When to use

Use when the user needs to communicate technical work to people who do not work with the system.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for formal published documentation; use technical-writing instead.

## Inputs required

- The audience and their prior knowledge
- The message and the outcome wanted
- The channel and the deadline

## Procedure

1. Lead with what changed for the reader, not with the component that changed.
2. Remove the system names, the version numbers and the internal terminology.
3. Explain the effect in terms of time, cost, risk or convenience.
4. Say what you are doing next and what the reader needs to do, if anything.
5. Keep the detail in a linked document for anyone who asks for it.

## Output contract

A message ready to send, with the ask, the context and the next step made explicit.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The ask is stated in one sentence
- [ ] The tone matches the audience and channel
- [ ] The next step and its owner are clear

## Safety notes

Never send a message on the user's behalf without explicit approval of the final text.
