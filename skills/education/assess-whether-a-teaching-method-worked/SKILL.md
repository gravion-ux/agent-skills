# Assess a teaching method by comparing what changed in the learners' work, not in the room

## Purpose

Assess a teaching method by comparing what changed in the learners' work, not in the room.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to know whether a way of teaching actually helped.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for self-directed study; use learning-skills instead.

## Inputs required

- The learner group and their level
- The learning objectives
- The session format and the time available

## Procedure

1. Write what the method was supposed to change in the learners' work.
2. Compare the work before and after using the same task or the closest equivalent.
3. Look at where the learners got stuck rather than only at the final score.
4. Ask the learners what they found hard and what they would do differently.
5. Decide whether to keep, change or drop the method, and write down why.

## Output contract

A lesson plan with objectives, activities, materials and an assessment.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every objective has an activity and an assessment
- [ ] The difficulty matches the learner level
- [ ] Materials are ready before the session

## Safety notes

Never use copyrighted source material without permission, and never grade a person without stating the rubric.
