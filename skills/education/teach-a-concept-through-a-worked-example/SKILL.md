# Teach a concept with a worked example that shows every step and then asks for a new one

## Purpose

Teach a concept with a worked example that shows every step and then asks for a new one.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to teach a procedure or a process by demonstration.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for self-directed study; use learning-skills instead.

## Inputs required

- The learner group and their level
- The learning objectives
- The session format and the time available

## Procedure

1. Choose an example the learners will recognise, but that is not their actual task.
2. Solve it step by step, saying the reasoning at each step rather than only the result.
3. Mark where a common mistake would occur and show how it is caught.
4. Give a near-identical problem and ask the learners to do the next step alone.
5. Give a different problem for independent practice afterwards.

## Output contract

A lesson plan with objectives, activities, materials and an assessment.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every objective has an activity and an assessment
- [ ] The difficulty matches the learner level
- [ ] Materials are ready before the session
