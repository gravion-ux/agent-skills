# Write ad copy that fits the channel's format and spends its first line on the hook

## Purpose

Write ad copy that fits the channel's format and spends its first line on the hook.

## When to use

Use when the user wants copy for a specific advertising channel.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a product implementation plan; use business-ops instead.

## Inputs required

- The product and its differentiator
- The audience and the buying stage
- The proof points and the constraints on claims

## Procedure

1. Check the character or format limits for that channel and write inside them.
2. Open with the most specific thing you can say about the audience's situation.
3. Keep one idea per ad, since the format rewards focus over coverage.
4. Match the call to action to where the ad actually sends people.
5. Write two or three variants so the team can test rather than guess.

## Output contract

A copy or campaign asset with a clear offer, a call to action and factual claims only.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every claim is backed by evidence
- [ ] The call to action names one next step
- [ ] The asset fits the channel format

## Safety notes

Never invent statistics, testimonials or endorsements, and never make a pricing claim without approval.
