# Negotiate a contract term by knowing what matters to both sides and trading on it

## Purpose

Negotiate a contract term by knowing what matters to both sides and trading on it.

## When to use

Use when the user is negotiating a clause in a customer or supplier contract.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a product implementation plan; use business-ops instead.

## Inputs required

- The product and its differentiator
- The audience and the buying stage
- The proof points and the constraints on claims

## Procedure

1. Write the term as it stands and state why the other side wants it.
2. Identify the underlying concern behind the term, which is often not the term itself.
3. List what the business can genuinely give without changing the deal's value.
4. Propose two options so the negotiation can move rather than stall on one position.
5. Get the final wording approved by the person who can accept it.

## Output contract

A copy or campaign asset with a clear offer, a call to action and factual claims only.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every claim is backed by evidence
- [ ] The call to action names one next step
- [ ] The asset fits the channel format

## Safety notes

Never invent statistics, testimonials or endorsements, and never make a pricing claim without approval.
