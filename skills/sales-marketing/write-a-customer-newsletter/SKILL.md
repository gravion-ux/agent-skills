# Write a customer newsletter around one useful idea rather than a list of announcements

## Purpose

Write a customer newsletter around one useful idea rather than a list of announcements.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to send a regular update to existing customers.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a product implementation plan; use business-ops instead.

## Inputs required

- The product and its differentiator
- The audience and the buying stage
- The proof points and the constraints on claims

## Procedure

1. Pick one topic that is useful to a customer regardless of what the business is selling.
2. Write the practical tip first, so a reader gets value before any announcement.
3. Keep the announcements to two or three items, each with a link.
4. End with the one action or offer relevant to the topic of the issue.
5. Keep the whole thing readable in under four minutes.

## Output contract

A copy or campaign asset with a clear offer, a call to action and factual claims only.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every claim is backed by evidence
- [ ] The call to action names one next step
- [ ] The asset fits the channel format

## Safety notes

Never invent statistics, testimonials or endorsements, and never make a pricing claim without approval.
