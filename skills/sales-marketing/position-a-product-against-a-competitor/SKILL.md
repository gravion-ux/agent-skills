# Position a product against a competitor by comparing what each one is built for

## Purpose

Position a product against a competitor by comparing what each one is built for.

## When to use

Use when the user needs to explain how the product differs from an alternative without disparaging it.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a product implementation plan; use business-ops instead.

## Inputs required

- The product and its differentiator
- The audience and the buying stage
- The proof points and the constraints on claims

## Procedure

1. List the buyers who choose each option and the constraints they have.
2. Identify what each product genuinely does better and where it genuinely does worse.
3. Choose the two or three differences that matter to the buyer you are speaking to.
4. Write the comparison so it reads as guidance rather than as an attack.
5. Keep every factual claim checkable and remove anything unverifiable.

## Output contract

A copy or campaign asset with a clear offer, a call to action and factual claims only.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every claim is backed by evidence
- [ ] The call to action names one next step
- [ ] The asset fits the channel format

## Safety notes

Never invent statistics, testimonials or endorsements, and never make a pricing claim without approval.
