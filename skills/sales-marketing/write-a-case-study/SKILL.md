# Write a case study around one customer's problem, the result and the evidence

## Purpose

Write a case study around one customer's problem, the result and the evidence.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to turn a customer outcome into a reusable story.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a product implementation plan; use business-ops instead.

## Inputs required

- The product and its differentiator
- The audience and the buying stage
- The proof points and the constraints on claims

## Procedure

1. Pick one customer with a result that is specific and that they have approved.
2. Write the problem in the customer's terms, including what it cost them.
3. Describe what was done in enough detail that a reader can recognise their own situation.
4. Give the result with the numbers and the time period they apply to.
5. Add the customer's quote, with their name and role, only after they approve it.

## Output contract

A copy or campaign asset with a clear offer, a call to action and factual claims only.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every claim is backed by evidence
- [ ] The call to action names one next step
- [ ] The asset fits the channel format

## Safety notes

Never invent statistics, testimonials or endorsements, and never make a pricing claim without approval.
