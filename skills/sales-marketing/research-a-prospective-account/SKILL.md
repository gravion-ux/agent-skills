# Research a prospective account using public sources before the first conversation

## Purpose

Research a prospective account using public sources before the first conversation.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to prepare to speak to an organisation they have not worked with before.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a product implementation plan; use business-ops instead.

## Inputs required

- The product and its differentiator
- The audience and the buying stage
- The proof points and the constraints on claims

## Procedure

1. Find what the organisation does, who it serves and how it is structured.
2. Look for the recent signals, such as hiring, funding, launches or leadership changes.
3. Identify the people most likely to own the problem the product addresses.
4. Check for a current supplier in the same area and what they appear to cover.
5. Write the three assumptions you will test on the first call.

## Output contract

A copy or campaign asset with a clear offer, a call to action and factual claims only.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every claim is backed by evidence
- [ ] The call to action names one next step
- [ ] The asset fits the channel format

## Safety notes

Never invent statistics, testimonials or endorsements, and never make a pricing claim without approval.
