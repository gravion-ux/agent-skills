# Break a research question into searchable subquestions with coverage and priority

## Purpose

Break a research question into searchable subquestions with coverage and priority.

## When to use

Use when the user needs a comprehensive but ordered set of questions to investigate.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. Identify the core question, its key concepts, and the required outcome.
2. Divide the question into subquestions that cover distinct evidence needs.
3. Prioritise each subquestion by decision relevance and evidence availability.
4. Attach search concepts and an expected evidence type to each subquestion.
5. Combine the subquestions into a coverage map with no major gaps.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
