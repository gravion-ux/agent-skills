# Frame a research question with scope, decision context, and measurable boundaries

## Purpose

Frame a research question with scope, decision context, and measurable boundaries.

## When to use

Use when the user needs to turn a broad interest into a bounded question for a decision.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. State the decision the research will inform.
2. Name the population, geography, and time period in scope.
3. Define the key terms and the evidence needed to answer the question.
4. List plausible answers and the observations that would distinguish them.
5. Rewrite the question so it can be answered within the available access and time.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
