# Write a literature review organised by themes, evidence, and unresolved questions

## Purpose

Write a literature review organised by themes, evidence, and unresolved questions.

## When to use

Use when the user needs a structured account of prior research and its gaps.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. Define the review question, scope, search method, and inclusion criteria.
2. Group studies into themes and explain the organising logic.
3. Synthesise evidence within each theme and compare methods and populations.
4. Trace citations for every factual claim and surface contradictions.
5. End with research gaps, limitations, and implications for the decision.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
