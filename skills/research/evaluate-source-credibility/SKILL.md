# Evaluate source credibility by checking provenance, evidence, expertise, and transparency

## Purpose

Evaluate source credibility by checking provenance, evidence, expertise, and transparency.

## When to use

Use when the user needs to judge how much weight a research source should receive.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. Trace the source to its publisher, author, and original evidence.
2. Check methods, data provenance, recency, and relevant conflicts.
3. Compare the claim against independent primary sources where possible.
4. Assign a confidence level and record the reason for that rating.
5. Use the rating to decide how much weight the claim receives.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
