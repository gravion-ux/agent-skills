# Read a research paper efficiently by extracting its design, evidence, and limits

## Purpose

Read a research paper efficiently by extracting its design, evidence, and limits.

## When to use

Use when the user needs the core content and evidential limits of an academic paper.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. Read the abstract, conclusion, and section headings to map the argument.
2. Identify the research question, design, sample, and measures.
3. Extract results with effect sizes, uncertainty, and supporting table references.
4. Check whether the methods support each main claim.
5. Record limitations, replication evidence, and three follow-up questions.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
