# Take structured research notes that preserve citations, claims, and open questions

## Purpose

Take structured research notes that preserve citations, claims, and open questions.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to capture source details without losing context or traceability.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. Create a note block with source metadata, reading date, and research question.
2. Summarise the source's purpose, method, sample, and principal findings.
3. Record each important claim with a page-linked citation.
4. Capture disagreements, caveats, and questions for later verification.
5. End the notes with three implications for the research synthesis.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
