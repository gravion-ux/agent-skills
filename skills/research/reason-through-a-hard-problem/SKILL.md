# Reason through a hard problem by decomposing it, testing competing hypotheses against weighed evidence and steelmanned objections, and concluding with calibrated confidence

## Purpose

Reason through a hard problem by decomposing it, testing competing hypotheses against weighed evidence and steelmanned objections, and concluding with calibrated confidence.

## When to use

Use when the user needs a careful, defensible answer to a complex or uncertain question where a first impression may be wrong.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for a quick fact check with one authoritative source; use communication instead.

## Inputs required

- The question and why it matters
- The scope, the date range and the sources allowed
- The decision the research informs

## Procedure

1. Restate the problem as one precise question and separate what is known, what is assumed and what is unknown before reasoning further.
2. Decompose the question into the sub-questions and causal factors that must each be resolved for an answer to hold.
3. Generate at least three competing hypotheses, including one that contradicts the initially favoured explanation.
4. Weigh the available evidence for and against each hypothesis, rating source quality and naming the evidence that is missing.
5. Steelman the strongest opposing hypothesis and list the assumptions the favoured hypothesis depends on, with a check for each assumption.
6. Decide under the remaining uncertainty by choosing the best-supported hypothesis and naming the observation that would change the conclusion.
7. State the conclusion with a calibrated confidence level, the key caveats and the next evidence that would most reduce uncertainty.

## Output contract

A written synthesis with claims linked to sources, the disagreements surfaced and the confidence stated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source
- [ ] Sources are dated and primary where possible
- [ ] Contradictions between sources are stated

## Safety notes

Never cite a source that was not opened, and never present an unverified claim as established.
