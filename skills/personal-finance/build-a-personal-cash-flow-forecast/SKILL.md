# Build a personal cash-flow forecast that projects income and committed spending month by month so a shortfall is visible while there is still time to act

## Purpose

Build a personal cash-flow forecast that projects income and committed spending month by month so a shortfall is visible while there is still time to act.

## When to use

Use when the user wants to see whether their money covers the coming months, including irregular bills and planned large expenses.

## When NOT to use

Do not use this for company accounting or tax filing; use finance-accounting instead.

## Inputs required

- The income, the bills and the goals
- The time horizon and the risk tolerance
- The jurisdiction for tax and benefit rules

## Procedure

1. List the income that is certain enough to count, with the date each payment actually lands in the account.
2. List every committed outflow by date: rent, bills, subscriptions, debt payments and insurance premiums.
3. Add the irregular and annual costs — tax, renewals, repairs and gifts — in the months they will really be paid.
4. Project the running balance month by month for six to twelve months using only those dated entries.
5. Mark every month where the balance dips below the user's safety floor and name the entries that cause the dip.
6. Fix the worst dips by moving a discretionary expense, building a sinking fund or renegotiating a due date, then re-run the projection.
7. Refresh the forecast monthly with actual figures and note which assumptions were wrong, so the next forecast starts from reality.

## Output contract

A prioritised plan with a budget breakdown, an emergency buffer target and the next concrete action.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The plan balances on the real numbers
- [ ] High-interest debt is addressed first if it exists
- [ ] Tax or benefit claims are flagged for verification

## Safety notes

Never give personalised tax or investment advice as if it were professional advice, and never ask for account numbers or credentials.
